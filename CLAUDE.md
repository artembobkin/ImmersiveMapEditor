# Immersive Map Editor

The desktop tool for the content of the ImmersiveMap engine (`~/Desktop/ImmersiveMap`, a Swift and Metal map engine), native to the Mac. It is meant to grow into the place where the engine's content is made and tuned (decided with the user, 2026-10-24). Today it is two things in one Swift package:

- **The tile pipeline** (`TileKit`, its commands run as `ImmersiveMapEditor pipeline COMMAND`, by the editor itself or from the terminal: one app, one binary, decided with the user, 2026-10-25): OpenStreetMap to one 3D model per z15 map tile (GLB with a palette and a baked light map), which the engine shows over its vector tile. Its rules of style and output are in `~/Desktop/TilePipeline/CLAUDE.md`: read it before changing what a tile looks like.
- **The editor** (the app), on its data folder (`~/Library/Application Support/Immersive Map Editor Data` by default, picked in Settings; `tiles/<z>/<x>/<y>/` a folder a tile with `osm/`, `roads/` and `build/`, then `landmarks/` and `config/`), one tile at a time:
  - **Choosing**: the map, OpenStreetMap under the grid of z15 tiles, each coloured by what the data folder has of it, or, by the Tiles/Engine tabs in the toolbar, where the views' tabs are once a tile is open (`Session.mapEngine`; the user, 2026-11-04), the engine's own map with the whole model archive over it, every built tile as the engine will show it, the camera handed over both ways and Update the Archive there too (the user, 2026-11-02); a tile is picked on the tiles' map, the panel lists every run of Claude of the session whatever its tile, going, queued and ended, each with its tile, state and time, a stop on each and Stop All, a row opening its tile and landmark (`Session.open(tile:landmark:)`, the landmark picked once the tile's OSM is read; the user, 2026-11-04: runs started in many tiles, nowhere seen together), and several are marked with shift-clicks, their OSM fetched at once by the panel's Fetch OSM for N Tiles, one job of the data folder with a fetch a tile that goes on past a tile Overpass refuses (`PipelineJob` with `goOn`, its `results` a command each with the pipeline's last word on a failure), the panel showing the fetch as it goes, which tile of how many, and as it came out, how many fetched and which failed and why (`Session.chosen`, `pick(_:adding:)`, `fetchChosen`; the user, 2026-11-04: one tile at a time was no way to get data, and the fetching was not to be understood from the log). A tile picked is opened: its OSM fetched and its road document seeded when missing, then its views, Buildings first (the tabs: Buildings, Roads, Style, Preview, Engine, ⌘1 to ⌘5; the user, 2026-11-01 and 2026-11-02).
  - **Roads**: corrects the tile's road document by hand, the one source of its roads (its format is the doc comment of `Sources/TileKit/Roads.swift`). Hidden in the first version (`Features.roads` false, `Session/Features.swift`, with `Features.lighting` for the Preview's light; the user, 2026-11-05: the landmarks' models alone): no Roads tab (`ViewMode.shown`), no seeding when a tile opens, no roads part in the build sheet and `--no-roads` on every build, no road state in the Tile section; the code stays for later.
  - **Tiles 3D**: the tile's model, its palette alone, built without its light (`pipeline build --no-light`).
  - **Preview**: what the engine will show, lit. The lighting is hidden from the panel for the first version (`LightSections.lightingShown` false: no shading, modifiers, sun, quality, light map or Bake; the build still bakes the light with the standard settings; the user, 2026-11-05: the tiles exported and archived, the light later). The tile built, by its light map (or the light alone), and its bake: sky light with bounces or ambient occlusion, a sun baked or none (`pipeline bake`). Or all the tile's landmarks' models together where the build places them, their palette alone, no build (`LandmarkView.Source.models`, placed by `Tile.metres` as `Imported` places them), the Subject "All the landmarks", with Rebuild Landmarks from Recipes, every model of the tile with a recipe built again as one job of the tile (`Session.rebuildLandmarks`; the user, 2026-11-02: a quick look at every model after a change of the style or a recipe); a modifier changed while the tile is shown bakes its light again a moment later, once more after the bake when it changed meanwhile (`Session.lightSettingsChanged`, on `LightSettings.previewKey`, whose modifiers JSON has sorted keys: JSONSerialization orders a dictionary's keys as it likes from one call to the next, and the key read that as a change, baking without end; the user, 2026-11-02: the modifiers seemed dead on the tile, then the bake never stopped). Or one landmark of the tile alone, without a build, with a Lens slider (the camera's vertical field of view, the engine's 45°, the camera moved so the model keeps its size: `LandmarkView.lens`) (the user, 2026-10-27: the models were judged on the palette alone, and half of Zenly's softness is light): `LandmarkLight` of TileKit reads its model, paints each face in its palette tone sided by the sun as the tile build does, bakes its light map with the same settings over a white ground at the tile's own texel (0.67 m: finer would hide the bake's noise the tile has, and showed it as streaks), and the view draws palette × light × the engine's sun shadow (a shadow map from the sun, the engine's cool tint, its strength a slider, since the engine's own is a zoom curve). It bakes again a moment after a setting changes. `--landmark-views` and `--landmark-look` take `--lit` for the same without a window.
  - **Landmarks**: the tile's landmarks, its own `landmarks/<id>/` folders (made in Buildings), each with its model when a GLB is in its folder. The pipeline places exactly these models, nothing from outside the app's data folder (the user, 2026-10-25: the app holds everything; how a landmark's model is made is still open).
  - **Engine**: the engine itself, ImmersiveMap linked at its main branch (`Package.swift`, `branch: "main"`, the user, 2026-11-05: the latest engine, no release tags between the two; `swift package update` takes a new commit of it, and `EngineView.engineVersion` says "main"), its own map with the data folder's model archive over it (`.modelArchive` of a file URL, the engine reads it from disk), so a tile is seen as it will really look, basemap, labels and the engine's light and all (the user, 2026-11-02). Update the Archive (⌘B, `TileAction.engine`) writes every built tile into a new `engine/models-STAMP.pmtiles` of the data folder and drops the older ones (a new file, so the engine's disk cache of the old one is never taken for the new), the first one by itself when the view opens with none; the camera starts over the tile open, from the south, tilted (`Session.engineStart`), the start kept in the view's own state for its life (the position given to the map must not follow the live camera, or every frame of a gesture is answered with a jump back: the tilt stuttered, the user, 2026-11-02), is remembered while another view shows (`Session.engineCamera`), is held to one tile around the tile open (`Session.engineBounds`, the engine's `cameraBounds`, elastic, pulled in across zooms 11 to 13; free on the map a tile is chosen on), and Centre on the Tile puts it back; the camera may come in to zoom 20 against the engine's own cap of 18 (`EngineView.maximumZoom`, `cameraSettings` applied before `cameraBounds`; the user, 2026-11-05: the camera stopped short of the houses); a Labels toggle in the panel turns the engine's labels off and on (`EngineView.labelsKey`, the engine's `labels(isEnabled:)`, live; the user, 2026-11-05: the house numbers in the way of the models); the engine's ground relief is off in this view: it is for looking at the models, which the pipeline builds on a flat ground, and the relief, collapsed at zoom 13.7 anyway, only gets in the way zoomed out (the user, 2026-11-03). `Views/Engine/EngineView`, `Inspector/EngineSections`. The engine's code is never changed from here, and nothing of the editor's own 3D views changes for it.
  - **Buildings**: the tile's OSM buildings and parts from above, picked to see all their tags and made a landmark candidate: the tile's own `landmarks/<id>/`, `landmark.json` (outlines in WGS84, parts, heights, every tag) and `footprint.geojson`, what a detailed model is made from (by hand, a tool, an AI). A candidate changes nothing of the build until a model of it is placed.

Public repository: `github.com/artembobkin/ImmersiveMapEditor`, MIT, as the engine.

## How we work

These are the user's decisions (2026-10-24). Follow them without asking again.

- **Swift only.** Everything is written in Swift: the app, the pipeline, the tools, the scripts. No Python, in any form: not for the build, not for a quick check, not for a one-off conversion (a throwaway check is a small Swift executable or a `swift` script in the scratchpad, never left in the package). No Blender: the user removed it. No JavaScript or web views.
- **Native to the Mac, modern Apple only.** The interface is SwiftUI with its own app life cycle: `@main App`, scenes (`WindowGroup`, `Window`, `Settings`), `.commands` for menus and shortcuts, `.toolbar`, `.inspector`, `.fileImporter`, `.alert`, `Canvas` for 2D drawing. State is Observation (`@Observable`, `@Bindable`, `@Environment`), work is Swift concurrency (`async`/`await`, `Task`, `@MainActor`). The GPU work (drawing, the light bake) is Metal. Forbidden, as old: `NSApplication` delegates and run loops, `NSWindow`, `NSMenu`, `NSAlert`, `NSOpenPanel`, `NSTextView`, `NSHostingView`, AppKit drawing (`NSView.draw`, `NSBezierPath`, `NSImage` drawing), Combine (`ObservableObject`, `@Published`, `sink`), `DispatchQueue`, completion handlers where an `async` API exists. **Where AppKit stays**: only in the folder `Sources/ImmersiveMapEditor/Metal/`, and only for what SwiftUI has no API for: the Metal views (`MTKView`) with their input (scroll wheel, right and other mouse buttons, key up), `MetalHost` (the one `NSViewRepresentable`), the `onScrollWheel` modifier for a SwiftUI view (a local `NSEvent` monitor), and the activation policy of the bare executable (`Platform`). No file outside `Metal/` imports AppKit: a new need for AppKit gets a small bridge there, used from SwiftUI.
- **Libraries.** A Swift library first. A C or C++ library is allowed when nothing in Swift does the job well, wrapped in Swift. GEOS (C++) was one and osm2streets (Rust) a tool: both are ported (2026-10-25), and Manifold (C++) too (2026-11-03), and live in packages of their own, SwiftTopology (`Topology`), SwiftStreets (`Streets` and what it stands on) and SwiftManifold (`Manifold`), so the build has no C, C++, Rust or other non-Swift code (the engine aside). A change to a port is made in its own repository (`~/Desktop/SwiftTopology`, `~/Desktop/SwiftStreets`, `~/Desktop/SwiftManifold`, each with its `CLAUDE.md`), tagged, and the version here raised. Each library, wrapper or port is a **target of its own** (`Geometry` on `Topology`, `GLTF`, `LightBaker`), never mixed into `TileKit` or the app. Name a wrapper's operations as the code it ports named them, so a port reads side by side with its original. Add a package dependency only for such a library, pinned to an exact version.
- **No non-Swift tools.** osm2streets, the one exception until 2026-10-25, is ported (`Streets`) and runs in process when a road document is seeded. Its Rust binary stays in the workspace (`tools/osm2streets_cli`) only as the live oracle of SwiftStreets' parity tests (its `OSM2STREETS_CLI`). Whether to delete it is the user's decision. Do not add non-Swift tools.
- **A port is faithful.** The same algorithm, constants, order of operations and tie-breaking as the original, then checked against it on real data (see Checking). Improve after the port is checked, not during it.
- **The engine is read, never changed** from here (`~/Desktop/ImmersiveMap`). It is linked as a package dependency at its main branch (the user, 2026-11-05; before, an exact release tag) (the Engine view shows its map; `make_app.sh` copies the packages' resource bundles, the engine's shaders and overview archive, into the app), and a target name must be unique across the package graph: SwiftStreets' earcut is `StreetsEarcut` for that. What the editor shows follows how the engine draws: when the engine's drawing of a model tile changes (`ImmersiveMap/Sources/ImmersiveMap/SceneModels/ModelTiles/`), the 3D view follows. The 3D camera works as the engine's (`UI/Gestures/MapGestureControllerMac.swift`): left button drags the ground, right button (or option with the left) turns sideways and tilts up and down, scrolling zooms toward the ground under the cursor. Keep the two alike.
- **Hand edits are sacred.** A road document in `roads/` holds the user's corrections: never seed it again, overwrite or "regenerate" it without being asked (`pipeline seed` refuses without `--force`). The editor writes it byte for byte in the pipeline's layout: an unedited document saved by the editor is identical to the file.
- **Sources are fixed.** `data/` is raw, never edited by hand. The OSM on disk (`osm/osm.bin`, Overpass's JSON parsed once into TileKit's binary and deleted, the user, 2026-11-03) is kept unless the user asks to fetch again.
- **OpenStreetMap's tile server**: only what is on screen, with the app's User-Agent, cached on disk, attributed on the map. Never fetch tiles in bulk.

## Writing

- English in every file: code, comments, logs, documentation, commit messages. Replies to the user are in Russian.
- No em dash (U+2014) and no semicolon joining two clauses in prose: two sentences, a comma, a colon or parentheses.
- Comments say what a thing is and why, in full sentences, in the density of the code around them. Every file starts with a doc comment of what it is for.

## Checking

Every change is checked before it is called done, and the check is said plainly (what ran, what came out).

- **Explanations only on hover** (the user, 2026-10-30): a panel shows controls and state (names, counts, what is picked, a date), never a paragraph explaining a control. What a control does goes in its `.help()`, shown when the pointer rests on it. A status line is state, not an explanation.
- **The landmarks' agent is shown, not lectured** (the user, 2026-10-26): what helped its models was what it sees itself (the contact sheets, the model beside the photo from the photo's own camera, the satellite view with the grid); a list of rules fed back after each build (the physical checks: what floats, what leads nowhere) made the models worse, so they were turned off and then removed (the user, 2026-10-28). Prefer a new way for the agent to see over a new rule for it to obey. (A second case: a list of "parts easy to miss" in get_landmark turned the Bolshoi's open galleries into solid annexes with windows, so it went too.)
- **The proxy never enters the repository** (the user, 2026-10-25): Claude Code runs through the user's proxy, kept in the keychain by Settings (`ClaudeSettings`), handed to `claude` in its environment. No proxy address, user or password in code, files of the project, defaults, logs or commit messages; never print it.
- **Never launch the editor or any app to check a change** (the user, 2026-10-25): the user launches and tries it. No smoke runs, no `--snapshot` or other headless runs of the editor on your own.
- **Never run the tests unless the user asks for it** (the user, 2026-10-25): no `swift test` of any kind on your own. Write the code and build it (`swift build`, no errors and no new warnings). When the user asks for the tests, `swift test` passing. The parity tests are much faster optimised: `swift test -c release -Xswiftc -enable-testing` (the test targets use `@testable import`).
- The parity tests of the ports are in their own repositories (SwiftTopology against GEOS 3.13's stored answers and the JTS XML suites, SwiftStreets against the Rust's stored and live output). Here `GeometryTests` checks the wrapper against GEOS's answers, stored before GEOS left the package (phase 12, `Docs/PortingParity.md`, and they cannot be made again). In git, `Tests/GeometryTests/.oracle/` (a hidden folder, so SwiftPM leaves it out of the target, read with `#filePath`): the non-finite inputs through the wrapper. Outside git, beside the recordings (`.build/recording/expected/`): the answers to every recorded call of five tiles and a seeding. The JTS XML suites are read from `.build/ports/jts` (or `JTS_ROOT`), the recordings from `.build/recording` (or `GEOMETRY_REPLAY`), and the tests that need them run when they are there. The long replays (overlays, predicates, distance, buffer, make valid and precision, and every recorded call through the wrapper, as recorded and on the grid of 0.01): `TOPOLOGY_LONG_REPLAY=1 swift test -c release -Xswiftc -enable-testing`, the whole package in about three and a half minutes. A whole tile's recorded calls timed against GEOS's stored times on this machine: `TOPOLOGY_SPEED=1 swift test -c release -Xswiftc -enable-testing --filter SpeedTests`. The answers of the recorded calls outside the wrapper are SwiftTopology's to replay (its `TOPOLOGY_RECORDINGS`).
- Looking at a result without a screen: `ImmersiveMapEditor --snapshot WORKSPACE OUT.png X/Y` (a tile in 3D) and `--map-snapshot WORKSPACE OUT.png` (the map) render offscreen to a PNG that can be read. Do not capture the screen (`screencapture`): it raises a permission prompt the user must answer. The live window is the user's to try.
- The road document: `ImmersiveMapEditor --resave IN OUT` then `cmp IN OUT`, identical.
- A tile: `pipeline validate`. A change to the pipeline: build `15/19808/10243` (Red Square, with landmarks, construction sites and roads) before and after and compare `stats.json` (`triangles`, `triangles_by_kind`, landmark windows, shelters) and the light map, and say what changed and why.
- Write builds of a check to the scratchpad (a copy of the tiles it needs), not to the data folder, unless the user asked for the tiles to be rebuilt.

## The package

Targets:

- `Geometry`: the build's geometry with shapely's names (buffer, union, difference, simplify, make valid, precision, offset curve, constrained Delaunay, polygonize, line merge, linear referencing, `Prepared`, `STRtree`) on `Topology`, the Swift port of JTS 1.20 that behaves as GEOS 3.13 (phase 8 of `Docs/PortingGoal.md`, 2026-10-25: every recorded call and the whole pipeline the same as on GEOS, `Docs/PortingParity.md`). Each operation fails as GEOS's C API failed.
- `Topology` (package SwiftTopology, `github.com/artembobkin/SwiftTopology`): JTS 1.20.0 in Swift (EDL-1.0), changed where GEOS 3.13 differs, each change in its `Docs/PortingParity.md`.
- `GLTF`: glTF binaries read (landmark models, their textures through ImageIO) and written (the tile: one mesh, palette and light map embedded; a fitted model of several materials; a mesh of no vertices gets bounds of zeros, since infinities are no JSON and a tile built of parts it had none of made a GLB its validation could not read, the user, 2026-11-04).
- `LightBaker`: the light map's bake on the GPU, Metal ray tracing: the tile from straight above, every surface white, an even sky a little brighter toward the zenith, three bounces by default. Needs Apple silicon.
- `Streets` (package SwiftStreets, `github.com/artembobkin/SwiftStreets`): osm2streets, streets_reader and osm2lanes (rev `fc119c4`) in Swift, Apache-2.0, on the ports it stands on, each a target there: `StreetGeom` (geom), `GeoAlgorithms` (geo), `StreetsEarcut` (0.1.1: named for the package, since the engine has an `Earcut` target of its own and a target name is unique across the graph), `OSMReader`, `CountryBoundaries`, and `MuvLanes` (Muv's reading of lanes from tags, the one MPL-2.0 target: its files stay MPL). ## Architecture

Keep the code clean as it grows (the user asked for it, 2026-10-24):

- **One responsibility per type and per file.** A type that keeps state, reads input and draws is three types. A file past about 300 lines is a sign to split by responsibility, into a folder.
- **Math and rules are plain values**, apart from views, AppKit and Metal: cameras, transforms, hit tests, edits are structs or extensions that tests can run (`OrbitCamera`, `ViewTransform2D`, `RoadHitTest`, `RoadEdits`).
- **Views are thin.** A Metal view turns events into calls on a controller or a tool and draws through a renderer. A SwiftUI view reads state and calls the session or the editor.
- **Open for extension**: a new tool is a new `RoadToolHandler`, a new mode a new view and panel, not a new branch in a switch spread over files.
- **One source of truth.** A format, a constant or a rule lives once: the road document's format and the markings' constants are TileKit's, the app uses them.
- **No hidden globals.** What a function reads or writes comes in as a parameter (`WorkspacePaths`), not a mutable global.
- **Names** follow Swift's: types, targets, source folders and files in UpperCamelCase (a file named after the type it holds), everything else in lowerCamelCase, local constants too (no `A`, `X`). Shell scripts are lowercase (`make_app.sh`).
- **Tests** (`Tests/`, swift-testing) cover the plain parts. A new rule of geometry, editing or a camera comes with a test.

## Commands

```sh
swift build -c release            # or open ImmersiveMapEditor.xcworkspace in Xcode (the package alone, with its shared schemes): there is no .xcodeproj
# The editor signs itself at launch (App/CodeSigning) with the user's Apple Development certificate under one identifier, then runs again: the keychain (proxy, tokens) and the folder permissions trust it once and for good. Scripts/sign.sh signs a binary by hand
./make_app.sh 0.1.0               # build/Immersive Map Editor.app, version 0.1.0 (else the last tag), the engine's shaders compiled into its bundle's default.metallib (swift build only copies them), signed as a whole under com.artembobkin.ImmersiveMapEditor; installed by copying it to /Applications
tp() { .build/release/ImmersiveMapEditor pipeline "$@"; }   # the pipeline's commands: tp COMMAND, in the data folder or with --root
tp build 15 --tiles 19808/10243 19810/10243     # fetch, seed roads, build, validate (or Z X Y, or Z X0 Y0 X1 Y1)
tp build 15 --tiles 19808/10243 --no-light      # the model alone, its light map white until tp bake (the editor's Tiles 3D)
tp lab data/15_19808_10243 OUT [--region CX CY HALF | --whole] [--export] [--samples N] [--roads FILE]
tp fetch 15 19808 10243 [--force]
tp seed 15 19808 10243 [--force]                # a tile's road document, never over hand edits without --force
tp migrate ~/Desktop/TilePipeline               # an older workspace copied into the data folder
tp bake 15 --tiles 19808/10243 [--size 1024] [--samples 256] [--bounces 3] [--gamma 1.6] [--lit R G B] [--shade R G B]
tp validate tiles/15/19808/10243/build
tp fit detsky-mir
.build/release/ImmersiveMapEditor [DATA FOLDER]
.build/release/ImmersiveMapEditor --resave IN OUT
.build/release/ImmersiveMapEditor --snapshot WORKSPACE OUT.png X/Y
.build/release/ImmersiveMapEditor --map-snapshot WORKSPACE OUT.png
```

A built tile keeps what its light needs in its `build/bake/`: `scene.bin` (`BakeScene`: the uncut triangles with the margin and the neighbours' buildings, in doubles, the region's and the light map's bounds, the landmark ids and the statistics that do not depend on the light, LZFSE-compressed, about 2.4 MB for Red Square) and `settings.json` (`Lighting.Settings` of the last bake: `size` texels over the tile, `samples`, `bounces`, `lit`, `shade`, `gamma`, and the `light_map_px` they gave). `bake` reads them, changes the settings given (the rest stay as stored), bakes again and redoes the light map, the cut, TEXCOORD_1, `stats.json`, the GLB, `placement.json`, `metadata.json` and the validation, in about 10 s against 26 s for the build. A tile with no `bake/` fails: build it first. With the stored settings it gives the build's geometry and statistics byte for byte (but `seconds`, the bake's own time). The light map is the same but a few dozen bytes off by 1 or 2, as it is between two builds: the GPU bake itself is not exactly repeatable.

The package needs macOS 15 and Swift tools 6.0 (in the Swift 5 language mode). In Xcode, use the shared schemes in `.swiftpm/xcode/xcshareddata/xcschemes/` (`ImmersiveMapEditor`, `ImmersiveMapEditor-Package`), with code coverage on. The `ImmersiveMapEditor` scheme runs the editor in Release and tests in Debug: run in Debug, with no optimisation, reading a landmark's GLB of 90 thousand triangles took seconds and every view dragged, and since every job runs the editor's own binary, Claude's set_recipe of GUM took 322 s against 8 s in Release, a landmark modelled in 5 minutes instead of 2 (the user, 2026-11-03); a job's console says so first thing when the editor is a Debug build (`Console.begin`); for breakpoints, switch the Run action to Debug for the while. With no argument the app opens the workspace opened last, else `~/Desktop/TilePipeline`. Started from the Finder, macOS asks once for access to the Desktop folder.
